The engineering/audit phase is complete enough. I now want to begin the actual quantitative research this project was built for.

Please first re-read the current repo, the latest audit/report, and the research/literature file so you understand the methodology and data before doing analysis.

My core conjecture is:

Scheduled macroeconomic releases may produce statistically repeatable and persistent FX price behavior beyond the immediate announcement shock, especially when the release is unusually surprising relative to expectations and/or meaningfully different from the previous reading.

I am specifically interested in persistence over subsequent complete H1 bars — hours to potentially multiple trading days — not seconds/minutes or news scalping.

Use the existing audited concepts of:

- Actual vs Forecast surprise
- Actual vs Previous momentum
- walk-forward percentile classification
- exact event-series identity
- normalized event-currency returns
- complete post-release H1 alignment

Do not assume the conjecture is true. Try to falsify it.

Start with cleaner, well-identified macro series and avoid unresolved/ambiguous series where appropriate.

Most importantly, design the research so we do not fool ourselves through data mining.

Before searching for patterns, establish an outcome-blind chronological Exploration vs Confirmation split. Use the Exploration period to understand event-response behavior and identify a small number of scientifically interesting candidate hypotheses. Freeze any candidate's rules before opening the Confirmation period.

Investigate questions such as:

- Do large macro surprises show persistent directionality after the first complete H1 candle?
- Does agreement between Surprise and Momentum matter?
- Do aligned +/+ or -/- states behave differently from contradictory states?
- Which event types show persistence, reversal, delayed response, or no effect?
- How stable are apparent effects across time, nearby horizons, simultaneous-release controls, and different FX pair representations?
- Are results visible in medians and individual-event behavior, or only in means driven by outliers?

Do not search thousands of combinations and simply return the best-performing one.

Account for multiple-testing / researcher-degrees-of-freedom risk.

Treat different FX pairs from the same underlying macro release carefully because they are not independent observations.

Report negative and inconclusive findings as seriously as positive ones.

The immediate objective is NOT to build a profitable trading strategy.

The objective is to answer:

“Does the historical data contain robust evidence of repeatable post-macroeconomic-release FX behavior that is strong and stable enough to justify a preregistered forward experiment?”

Produce a clear research report with:

1. methodology and Exploration/Confirmation split;
2. descriptive event-response findings;
3. strongest candidate phenomena and their weaknesses;
4. failed or unsupported hypotheses;
5. robustness / overfitting analysis;
6. confirmation results for any preregistered candidates;
7. a small number of frozen setup specifications suitable for future demo-account forward testing, but only if the evidence genuinely warrants them.

Use the existing application/data where possible and create additional research scripts/artifacts only where useful.

Do not manufacture an edge.

Do not optimize for attractive results.

If the conjecture is unsupported, say so clearly.

The goal is trustworthy evidence, not proof of what I hope is true.
