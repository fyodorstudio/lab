==================================================
PRIMARY RESEARCH CONJECTURE
==================================================

I want to test whether scheduled macroeconomic releases create statistically repeatable and persistent FX price behavior AFTER the immediate release shock.

I am NOT interested in scalping the first seconds/minutes.

The main conjecture is:

"When scheduled macroeconomic information is sufficiently surprising relative to expectations and/or sufficiently different from the previous reading, the affected currency sometimes exhibits persistent directional price behavior over subsequent complete H1 bars, potentially lasting several hours to multiple trading days."

More specifically, I suspect that persistence may be stronger when:

- Surprise A-F is unusually large relative to that exact event's historical distribution;
- Momentum A-P points in the same direction as Surprise;
- the event is economically important;
- the release is not badly confounded by contradictory simultaneous releases;
- the macro regime makes that category of information relevant.

I do NOT assume this conjecture is true.

Attempt to falsify it.

==================================================
NULL / BASELINE
==================================================

The practical null is approximately:

- median normalized post-event return ≈ 0;
- positive direction rate ≈ 50%;
- no stable directional persistence after the initial repricing;
- apparent patterns disappear across time, nearby horizons, or out-of-sample data.

==================================================
ROBUSTNESS
==================================================

For interesting exploratory patterns, check:

1. Mean versus median

If mean is large but median is close to zero, identify possible outlier domination.

2. Individual-event paths

Show whether the apparent effect comes from a handful of extreme releases.

3. Chronological stability

Split the exploration data into earlier/later portions.

4. Nearby horizons

If H12 looks strong, inspect nearby horizons such as H8-H16.

A credible phenomenon should not generally exist only at exactly one magical bar.

5. Nearby threshold definitions

Do NOT optimize thresholds yet.

Only as a robustness diagnostic, determine whether the qualitative result completely disappears around nearby percentile definitions.

6. Weekend paths

Compare all paths versus weekend-crossing paths where useful.

7. Non-weekend candle gaps

Flag observations containing known missing-market-data gaps.

8. Simultaneous-release sensitivity

Check whether the apparent effect survives isolated-event analysis.

==================================================

Do not rely only on means and direction percentages.

==================================================
CANDIDATE DISCOVERY
==================================================

The goal of Exploration is NOT to produce a trading strategy.

It is to identify a small number of candidate phenomena worth preregistering.

A candidate should ideally show:

- adequate N;
- nontrivial median behavior, not only mean;
- directional-rate displacement from 50%;
- a sensible H1-H42 path;
- limited dependence on one or two extreme events;
- reasonable chronological stability;
- reasonable nearby-horizon stability;
- survival under obvious contamination controls.

Do not automatically rank thousands of combinations and return "the best."

Instead explain the strongest scientifically interesting candidates and their weaknesses.

Also explicitly report when no credible candidate exists.
